## AI in higher education
How AI, especially generative AI and LLM tutors, changes university teaching, learning and assessment. Prioritize novice programmers and students learning to vibe code: how they specify intent, understand, verify and repair generated code, calibrate confidence, and regulate their learning. Distinguish faster task completion from durable understanding and transfer after AI support is removed. Favor controlled experiments, classroom deployments, longitudinal studies, and qualitative studies explaining who benefits and who struggles. Instructor perspectives, participatory co-design, scaffolding, learner models, and comparisons of tutoring approaches directly inform the dissertation. Academic integrity and course or institutional policy remain relevant.

- Venues: L@S, AIED, EDM, IJAIED, Computers & Education, Computers & Education: AI, IEEE TLT
- Avoid: K-12-only studies unless they provide strong empirical evidence about AI tutoring or learning transfer that could inform higher education; opinion pieces with no data or substantive theoretical or methodological contribution; productivity claims that equate faster completion with learning

## Agents and LLMs
How LLM agents plan, use tools and act over long tasks; agent frameworks, evaluation and benchmarks; multi-agent systems; reliability and failure analysis.

- Venues: NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, ACL, EMNLP, KDD
- Avoid: incremental benchmark scores with no new method or insight

## AI in HCI
How people use, trust, steer and collaborate with AI systems; interfaces for LLMs and agents; human-AI co-creation; user studies of AI tools, including developer tools. Prioritize the verification gap in AI-assisted programming: novices and non-CS domain experts can generate code they cannot reliably evaluate, and confidence can diverge from correctness. Look for evidence connecting interviews or self-reports to observed behavior, interaction logs, and audits of the software participants build or deploy. Non-programmers, scientists and analysts, trust calibration, intent specification, end-user programming, and verification support are especially relevant. Favor work that informs the vibe-coding interview study, instructor co-design, or deployment measuring learning and artifact quality. Accessibility and equity matter when they reveal verification barriers or unequal benefits. Notebook workflows and professional developer studies matter most when their mechanisms or methods transfer to these populations.

- Venues: CHI, CSCW, UIST, TOCHI
- Avoid: generic coding-assistant productivity comparisons with no insight into human behavior or artifact quality; security benchmarks with no connection to human verification, AI coding workflows or deployed software

## Context management in AI
How agents and LLM apps choose, compress, retrieve and carry context across sessions and tools; memory systems; context transfer between tools; long-context evaluation.

- Venues: NeurIPS, ACL, EMNLP
- Avoid: pure long-context architecture papers with no agent or app angle

## Multimodal and new kinds of models
New model families and modalities: vision-language, audio and video models, world models, new architectures beyond the standard transformer, and notable frontier model reports.

- Venues: NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR
