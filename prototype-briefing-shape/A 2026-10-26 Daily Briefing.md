## AI in higher education

### An AI Factory for Technology Education: An Agentic Pipeline with Closed Loop Audience Feedback
Hernandez, Amado-Salvatierra, Hernandez Rizzardini · arXiv [2610.31875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31875)

- [ ] Keep ^pick-2610-31875

**Why it's here:** course content generated by an agentic pipeline that is revised from what learners actually do, not written once and shipped.

A typed workflow graph treats tutorial and course production as a manufacturing line, with audience feedback closing the loop. Aimed at professional upskilling, where tool guidance goes stale in weeks.

### MisconceptDialogue: Evaluation Criteria Shape Measured Success in LLM Math Tutoring
Lee, Woo, Kim, Gwon, Huang · arXiv [2610.31679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31679)

- [ ] Keep ^pick-2610-31679

**Why it's here:** asks whether the numbers reported for LLM tutors can be trusted, which bears on any evaluation of AI tutoring.

Five open-weight tutors (7B–14B) detect, diagnose and remediate student misconceptions over 5,000+ dialogues with simulated students; the headline is that the choice of evaluation criteria shapes measured success.

## Context management in AI

### Context or Knowledge: How LLMs Attribute Their Own Answers
Afendulev, Dontsov, Chepurova, Tutubalina, Korzni · arXiv [2610.32109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32109)

- [ ] Keep ^pick-2610-32109

**Why it's here:** probes whether a model knows an answer came from supplied context or its own weights, by editing the KV cache.

Changing or removing the document's cache never changes the model's source report across four models (7B–32B); transplanting the question-and-answer cache does.

### SPECMEM: Accelerating Agentic Tool Calling via Live Memory Management
Song, Zhao, Liu, Zhu, Xu, Li, Yu · arXiv [2610.31840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31840)

- [ ] Keep ^pick-2610-31840

**Why it's here:** cross-session memory of past tool calls, kept live and managed, used to speed up agents.

Retrieved past tool calls serve as speculative-decoding drafts, since each user's behaviour tends to repeat; existing systems keep no cross-session memory or never update it.
